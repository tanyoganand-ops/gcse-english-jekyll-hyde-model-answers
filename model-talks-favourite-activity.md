# Model talks: 'How I prefer to spend my time'

Original model answers written for the OCR-style writing task
"Write the words of a talk to young people about your favourite activity."
Both are fictional model answers.

MODEL ANSWER 1: Coding

Good morning, everyone. Today I want to talk about my favourite activity: coding.

In simple terms, coding means writing instructions that tell a computer what to do. With a few lines of text, you can build a game, a website or an app that other people use.

Most evenings I open my laptop and choose a small problem to solve. Perhaps I want a timer for revision, or a tool that sorts my notes. I plan it on paper first, then write the code step by step. It almost never works the first time. I read the error message, search for the cause, fix it and test again. Sometimes that cycle repeats twenty times before the program finally runs.

That is exactly why I love it. The frustration is real, but the moment something works is unbeatable. It feels like solving a puzzle that you built yourself. Coding has also made me more patient, because I have learned that a mistake is only information, not failure.

It matters to me for another reason too. When I make something useful, I am creating technology rather than only consuming it. That gives me confidence, and it has opened doors I never expected, such as projects with other young people online.

If you have never tried it, I would encourage you to start small. Many free tools exist, and you only need curiosity. Ten minutes a day is enough to begin.

Thank you for listening. Perhaps one of you will build something remarkable.


MODEL ANSWER 2: Running

Hello, everyone. My favourite activity is running, and I would like to explain why it means so much to me.

I run three or four times a week, usually early, before the streets wake up. I begin with a slow warm-up, then settle into a steady pace. On Saturdays I join a local group and we try a longer route, often five or six kilometres. I track my times, but I try not to obsess over them. Beating last month's version of myself is the only competition that counts.

Honestly, the first ten minutes are often hard. My legs feel heavy and my mind offers every excuse to stop. Then something changes. My breathing steadies, my thoughts clear, and the stress of the week fades away.

That feeling is what I value most. Running is my reset button. After a difficult day at school, a short run leaves me calmer, more focused and genuinely proud. I have also learned discipline: you do not need to feel motivated, you only need to put your trainers on.

There is a social side as well. Strangers at the group quickly become friends, because everyone is working towards something similar.

You do not need expensive equipment or any talent. Start with a gentle jog and walk when you must. Progress tends to be gradual.

Thank you for listening. I hope you will try it soon.
